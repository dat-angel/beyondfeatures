# The Discovery Grid: Finding Buyers When Research Is Agent-Mediated

**By [Beatriz Datangel Rodgers](https://www.linkedin.com/in/bzdata/)** 

**Context:** Fireside framework (Tech Week / founder GTM). **Beatriz Datangel Rodgers** and security product leader **Amber Bennoui** co-developed this for talks on agent-mediated buying.

**What this is:** A simple 2×3 grid so teams split “how we reach buyers” from “how buyers’ tools find us” — then ship one concrete improvement this week.

---

## The problem in one sentence

Your next buyer may never scroll LinkedIn first. They (or their org) use **AI assistants and agents** to research categories, compare vendors, and form a **shortlist** before your outbound or ads get a shot.

---

## The Discovery Grid

Think in two axes:

1. **Who is doing the discovery?** Your GTM motion vs **agents and assistants** acting on the buyer’s behalf.
2. **What era of tactics are you using?** What used to work vs what matters when summaries and recommendations are automated.

|  | **You finding buyers** | **Agents finding you** |
|--|------------------------|------------------------|
| **What worked before** | Outbound, paid acquisition, events, broad “content marketing” | Classic SEO, review sites, analyst reports, word of mouth |
| **What works now** | **Agent-informed ICP work** (challenge your assumptions with tools), prompt-based research, real community signal | **Structured, verifiable surface area:** docs that state reality clearly, consistent product/category language, machine-readable cues (for example schema on key pages), proof that tools can cite (changelog, incidents, security artifacts where relevant) |
| **What to build this week** | Ask an assistant: *“Describe the ICP for [your product]. Who is a bad fit?”* Compare to your internal doc. | Ask an assistant: *“What’s the best [category] tool for [specific use case]?”* See whether **you** appear. If not, note what the winners have that you don’t (usually: clarity + proof + structure, not more adjectives). |

---

## Why a “trust filter” matters (especially in security)

Agents and buyers do not only match **keywords**. They favor answers they can **defend**: public artifacts, coherent story across site and docs, evidence of maintenance, and security-adjacent signals when the category demands them.

You can be **findable** and still lose if you look credible in marketing copy but thin on **verification**.

---

## Takeaways

1. **Split the problem:** Human GTM vs agent-mediated discovery. Budget and ship for both.
2. **Run two prompts this week:** ICP stress-test + category shortlist test.
3. **Optimize for inspectability:** Clear positioning, structured pages, dated changelogs, real technical writing — fewer generic listicles.
4. **Pair marketing surface with proof:** Especially in security and dev tools, market visibility and technical trust compound; neither replaces the other.

---

## Optional: “Invisible startup” check (5 minutes)

1. Pick your primary category phrase (the one a buyer would type, not your internal codename).
2. Run the shortlist prompt in **two** different assistants.
3. Capture: Do you appear? Who does? What do top answers cite (docs, GitHub, reviews, comparisons)?
4. Pick **one** gap to close first (usually: one flagship doc page, one proof asset, or clearer “who it’s for / not for” on the homepage).
